## Two Villages and a Ridge

Colle and Fonte are eleven hundred metres apart across a wooded ridge in the Abruzzo and there is no road between them.

There was a road. It went over the top and it was a mule track that had been a mule track since the Romans, and it is still there under about fifty years of hornbeam and it is perfectly walkable if you know it, and Dario has walked it, and he is not supposed to have.

The road that exists now goes down from Colle to the valley and along and back up to Fonte and it is twenty-nine kilometres and takes fifty minutes.

Nobody makes that drive. That is the thing to understand about the geography: it is not that the two villages are far apart, it is that they are eleven hundred metres apart and might as well be in different countries, and the fifty minutes exists as a fact and has almost never been used.

Dario has been to Fonte twice in his life. Once at seven, for a funeral of somebody related through a marriage in the 1950s, in a car, with his mother, without his grandfather. And once at nineteen.

The thing that happened in 1974 was about water.

Dario would like to set out the whole of it, because everybody assumes it must have been something enormous. There is a spring on the Colle side of the ridge and it had fed both villages through a masonry channel since 1911, and in 1972 the comune of Colle put in a new tank and the channel was cut, and it was almost certainly not deliberate — the men who did it were from Pescara and they were laying a pipe — and Fonte was without water for eleven days in the August.

There was a meeting. It went badly. There was a second meeting that went worse, and there was a court case that ran until 1979 and that Fonte won, and by then it was not about water.

Dario has read the judgment. It is in the comune archive at Colle and it is fourteen pages and it is extremely boring, and the operative part is about who owns a masonry channel built in 1911 under an agreement that nobody could produce.

Fonte got the water back in the September of 1972, eleven days after it stopped. The whole of the actual problem lasted a week and a half.

Dario's grandfather has not spoken to anybody from Fonte since he was twenty-two.

He has spoken to people from Fonte. Dario has watched him do it — at a hospital in Sulmona, at a market, once at a wedding — perfectly civilly, about the weather, for two minutes at a time.

What he has not done in fifty-one years is have a conversation.

He is not a bitter man. That is the part Dario has never got anybody outside the valley to understand.

He tried it once on a girlfriend from Rome, who said, reasonably, that it sounded as though everybody should get over it, and Dario found that he could not construct a single sentence in his grandfather's defence and also could not agree with her. He is a cheerful, decent, extremely funny old man who has three friends in the valley and goes to everything and helps everybody, and if you say the word Fonte at his table the temperature goes down four degrees and stays there for the rest of the meal.

## The Wolf

There are wolves in that part of the Abruzzo — properly, verifiably, in numbers, since the protections came in — and everybody in both villages knows it and nobody makes a fuss.

The one on the ridge is different only in that she is regular.

She walks the old mule track. Not near it, not roughly along it: on it, the full length, from the last house at Colle to the last house at Fonte, most nights between October and March, and she has been doing it for something over eleven years.

There is nothing remarkable in a wolf using a track. It is the least remarkable thing about her. A wolf on the move will take the easiest line through cover exactly the way anything does, and a mule track cut into a hillside in the Roman period is, by construction, the easiest line over that ridge, and it does not stop being the easiest line because two comuni stopped speaking in 1974.

She is not walking between the villages. She is walking over the ridge, and the villages happen to be at the two ends of the only sensible way over it, which is why they are where they are.

Both villages know. Both villages have known for a decade. There are camera-trap photographs of her in the bar at Colle and there are camera-trap photographs of her in the bar at Fonte and they are, as far as Dario can establish, the same photographs, because they came off the same trap.

The trap belongs to the national park.

It is on a beech about halfway up on the Colle side, in a place that is technically in the comune of Colle by about sixty metres, and the park sends the images to both village halls because the park does not have a position on 1974 and has never been asked to develop one.

That is the joke, and both villages make it, separately, in exactly the same words, and neither of them knows the other one makes it.

Dario found the mule track at eleven because he was following her tracks in snow and did not think about where he was going until he came out at the top and saw the roofs of Fonte below him.

He stood there for a long time. He was closer to Fonte than to his own house. He had never in his life seen it from above.

It is a completely ordinary Abruzzese village. Twenty-odd roofs, a bell tower, a road going down, a football pitch with one net.

He had spent eleven years understanding Fonte as a word people did not say at his grandfather's table and it turned out to be twenty-odd roofs.

## The Girl on the Other Side

Her name is Sara and she was twelve and she was standing on the track about four hundred metres down the Fonte side with a phone in her hand, and she had been following the same tracks up.

They looked at each other for a while.

He has thought about that pause a great deal and he does not think either of them was frightened. What they were doing was arithmetic: two children on a track that neither village used, each working out which village the other was from and what that was supposed to mean, and both of them arriving at *I have no idea what I am supposed to do here* at about the same moment.

What Dario says about that moment, at twenty-four, is that he knew exactly who she was before she said anything, because there are two families in Fonte with that hair and one of them is the family from the court case.

Neither of them said hello.

What Sara said, eventually, holding up the phone, was: "Have you got her on anything?"

And Dario said no, and that his was a rubbish phone, and that she had gone past his uncle's shed at about half five.

Sara said that she had her going up past the chapel at twenty past six.

And the two of them stood on a mule track that neither village had used since 1974 and worked out, from two sightings fifty minutes apart, roughly how fast a wolf walks, which is about four kilometres an hour, which is a fact Dario has never forgotten and has never needed.

They did that for two winters.

Not often. Perhaps nine or ten times a year, in the snow, when the tracking was good, always on the track, always by accident to begin with and then, from about the second January, not by accident at all.

They did not become friends exactly. Dario wants to be precise about this because the story gets away from him otherwise. They were two children in different schools in different valleys who had one subject in common, and the subject was an animal, and they talked about the animal for a couple of hours a winter for two years and about almost nothing else.

They had no way to contact each other. That is a detail people find hard to believe now and it is central: neither of them ever gave the other a number, for two years, because the number would have had to be explained at some point to somebody.

So it worked like this. If it snowed, and the tracks were on the Colle side, Dario went up. If it snowed, and she could get out, Sara went up. Roughly a third of the time the other one was there.

He knows that her grandmother was in the 1974 meetings. She knows that his grandfather was. Neither of them ever raised it and Dario cannot now remember which of them decided that.

## What It Did Not Fix

Nothing was fixed.

Dario is twenty-four and his grandfather is eighty-two and has not spoken to anybody from Fonte, and the road is still twenty-nine kilometres, and the mule track is still under the hornbeam, and there is no version of this story in which two villages made it up because some children met a wolf.

He wants that stated plainly, because he has told this at university twice and both times somebody has tried to give it an ending.

The endings offered were: that the two villages should hold a joint festival, and that Dario and Sara should have got married. He is not making either of those up.

What happened is smaller and he thinks it is the only kind of thing that actually happens.

He told his grandfather about Sara when he was nineteen. He did it badly and at the wrong moment, at a table, with people there, and his grandfather went the four degrees down and said nothing for the rest of the meal, and then came and found him in the yard about an hour later and asked one question.

He asked what her grandmother's name was.

Not what Sara was like. Not what they had talked about. Not whether Dario had told anybody. One question, in a yard, an hour later, having plainly thought about nothing else during the meal.

Dario told him. His grandfather stood there for a bit and then said: "She was two years above me at the school in the valley. Before all this. She could sing."

And went in.

That is the whole of it and it did not lead to anything. There was no reconciliation and no visit and his grandfather has not said her name since.

But Dario is twenty-four and Sara is twenty-four and they are in touch about four times a year, and when his grandfather and her grandmother are gone there is going to be one family in Colle and one family in Fonte in which the word does not drop the temperature, and neither of those families will be able to point to a moment when that changed.

The wolf is dead or gone. The track stopped in the winter of 2019 and there is a photograph from the trap in the February of that year which is the last one, and eleven years is a good run for a wolf.

Both bars still have the photographs up. Dario checked the one in Fonte in 2023, on the second visit of his life, and it is in a frame by the door and it is the same image as the one in Colle and it has a different caption.

There is another one on the ridge now. She does not use the mule track.
